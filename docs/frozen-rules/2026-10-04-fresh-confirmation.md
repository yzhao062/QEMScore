# Frozen Rule: Fresh-Circuit Confirmation of the Descriptor-Information Results (QEMScore, Post-Review)

Written 2026-10-04 (US Pacific time), before any dataset of this rule was generated and before any fit. A seventh internal review panel (2026-10-04) observed that the earlier follow-ups were frozen before fitting but designed with earlier results in view. Their rungs, formula, thresholds, and candidate sets were chosen that way, so their tests are conditional calibration on reused datasets. This rule asks whether the main statements hold on circuits that did not exist when it was frozen.

Every result of the rules `2026-10-02-descriptor-information.md`, `2026-10-03-strength-indicator.md`, `2026-10-03-shot-sweep.md`, and `2026-10-03-strong-learners.md` was known when this rule was written. So were the post hoc analyses released with them: the validation-only ceiling, the stacking increments, and the joint bands. The predictions below were chosen to restate those results. Their thresholds were set from the earlier dataset seeds and were checked against them (section Locked Predictions). No fresh dataset seed has been generated, at any size, by any code.

## Question

The paper makes statements about the negative control, the positive controls, the descriptor ladder, the shot rule, and the stacking increment. Do they hold on three new dataset seeds, scored once under predictions locked here?

## Design

**Datasets.** Dataset seeds 401, 503, and 607, generated by `tools/fresh_panel.py generate --seeds 401 503 607 --size 640`. For each seed this calls `generate_split(campaign_split_spec("shipped", 640), <out>/regen-shipped-s<seed>-n640, master_seed=<seed>)`, the generator, noise model, families, sizes, and shot count of the campaign datasets. Each family (transverse-field Ising, Heisenberg) gets 640 training, 320 source-validation, and 160 test circuits, with depolarizing-plus-readout noise at L1 and L3 and 2,048 shots. The seeds were chosen before any generation and are not in `qemscore.campaign.design.SEEDS`. No seed is replaced, regenerated, or dropped, whatever its data look like.

**Determinism check.** `tools/fresh_panel.py verify --seed 401` regenerates seed 401 into a second directory and requires byte-identical items.jsonl. If it fails, no fit runs and the failure is reported.

**Shot levels.** As in the shot-sweep rule: 256, 1,024, 2,048, 8,192, 32,768, and 131,072 shots, plus the exact noisy expectation. `tools/shot_sweep.py generate` derives each level from the fresh 2,048-shot dataset by the sweep rule's generation path. The sweep rule's three checks apply: faithfulness (the regenerated 2,048-shot level must reproduce the fresh dataset byte for byte), the exact level, and the level caches. A failed check stops every fit of the affected seed.

**Machines.** Generation, validation, the three checks, and preparation run on the macOS machine that produced the earlier datasets, with qiskit 2.5.2, qiskit-aer 0.17.2, numpy 2.2.6, scipy 1.18.1, and scikit-learn 1.9.1. On aarch64 DeltaAI, one earlier dataset failed revalidation by 1e-12 in a stored label, so DeltaAI fits on the macOS files. They are identified by the SHA-256 values that `tools/fresh_panel.py` records in `fresh_dataset.json` and by the cache SHA-256 values that preparation records. Every fit runs on NCSA DeltaAI with the package versions above, and every worker sets OMP_NUM_THREADS, OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS, and MKL_NUM_THREADS to 1. The run log records the commit, versions, numpy's BLAS, `threadpoolctl.threadpool_info()`, and `blas_fpe_probe()` before the first fit.

**Preparation.** `tools/descriptor_ladder.py prepare --fresh` builds the 2,048-shot tree. It validates each dataset with `validate_split_artifact` and the structural assertions of the normal path, in place of the campaign-archive comparison that fresh seeds cannot have. Every other step is unchanged: caches, the N-rung coupling-noise draws, the R4 encoder cache, and diagnostics. `prepare --sweep --fresh --fresh-source <2,048-shot tree>` builds each other level's tree; its identifier and ideal-value checks compare against the fresh 2,048-shot dataset. The 2,048-shot tree alone holds the R4 encoder cache, because only that level fits R4; the level-cache check accepts this and records it (`--reference-only-encoder-cache`).

**Fit sets.** Every arm reads the noise-strength indicator of the 2026-10-03 rule.
- *Original candidates* (random forest and MLP, as in the 2026-10-03 strength-indicator rule). At 2,048 shots: rungs R0, N1, N2, N3, N4, R3-TFI, R3-Heis, R4, and R5. At the other six levels: R0, N1, N2, and R5.
- *Strong candidates* (`--strong-learners`, as in the 2026-10-03 strong-learner rule, with its derived baseline). The same rungs at the same levels.

Arms A, C, F, and P run at learner seeds 1 to 20 (A once per dataset, rung, level, and set). M is F at R5. Candidates, preprocessing, selection, schedules, the permuted column, and learner seeds are those of the earlier rules. The R0 reproduction gate is not a condition; the run passes the earlier record with `--gate-file artifacts/descriptor-information/gate/gate_r0.json`.

**Order of work.** The scripts are `artifacts/descriptor-information/round8/scripts/mac_fresh.sh` (steps 1 and 2) and `round8_stage.sh` (steps 3 to 7) beside it.
1. Generate the three seeds; run the determinism check; derive the six other levels with their checks.
2. Prepare every tree on macOS. `tools/fresh_panel.py record-caches` writes each tree's cache SHA-256, and a list holds the SHA-256 of every file copied to DeltaAI. On DeltaAI, every copied file must match that list, and every run passes `--cache-record`; it exits before the first fit on any difference.
3. Fit A and C of the original candidates at 2,048 shots at R0, N1, N2, and R5.
4. Write the predictions file (section Locked Predictions) with `tools/fresh_confirmation.py predict`. Record its SHA-256 and time in the run log before any fit of F or P.
5. Fit everything else, skipping fits already present.
6. Run both analysis scripts on every tree with `--dataset-seeds 401 503 607` and `--follow-up-rule` naming this file. They must agree to 1e-12 with identical labels and rung statements. Then run the reconcile script, the strong-learner derive script, learner adequacy, and the stacking script. The stacking script runs with `--unpinned`, because these fits are not yet in a release; every other input check applies.
7. Score the predictions once with `tools/fresh_confirmation.py score`. Run on the earlier seeds and their released analyses, the same script gives every earlier value quoted below.

## Estimands, Uncertainty, and Classification

These follow the earlier rules unchanged. D = C − F, and D/C is a ratio of means inside each draw. Intervals come from the two-stage percentile bootstrap: 10,000 draws, a fresh `numpy.random.default_rng(20261002)` per row and quantity, learner seeds then whole test circuits. The three labels are unchanged, and a rung statement needs all three dataset seeds to agree; otherwise it reads "mixed". In the paper the labels read "F beats C" (`measurement_adds`), "C beats F" (`measurement_hurts`), and "not distinguished". The relative stacking increment is that of `tools/stacking_increment_all.py`, with its two-stage interval.

## Locked Predictions

A **row** is one fresh dataset seed and family (six rows). A **cell** is one row, rung, shot level, and fit set. "Holds" below means the stated label or bound on every listed cell. Each prediction passes, fails, or is not testable, and each is scored once.

**P1, negative control (original candidates).** At R0, no row reads "F beats C" at any of the seven levels (42 cells). On the earlier seeds, none of the 42 did.

**P2, negative control (strong candidates).** At R0 and 2,048 shots, the upper limit of the D/C interval is below 0.01 on every row. On the earlier seeds the largest upper limit was 0.007.

**P3, descriptor ladder (original candidates, 2,048 shots).** Along R0, N1, N2, N3, N4, the first rung whose rung statement is "F beats C" is N2 for transverse-field Ising and N3 for Heisenberg. On the earlier seeds it was N2 and N3.

**P4, descriptor ladder (strong candidates, 2,048 shots).** The same first rungs as P3. On the earlier seeds they were N2 and N3.

**P5, positive controls (original candidates, 2,048 shots).** R3-TFI on the three transverse-field Ising rows, and R4 and R5 on all six rows, read "F beats C". The same holds for R5 at the six other levels. On the earlier seeds, every one of these cells did.

**P6, the shot rule locates the label change (original candidates, N1 and N2).** The predictions file gives, for each row, rung (N1, N2), and level, the ceiling D/C*_val = 1 − macro(c_comb) / macro(c_C^val), with c_comb = ((c_C^val)^−2 + (c_m^cf)^−2)^−1/2 in each rule cell. Here c_C^val is C's validation error per rule cell, the mean over learner seeds 1 to 20 of the fresh 2,048-shot C fits. The error c_m^cf is the two-fold cross-fitted error of the de-attenuated estimate on the level's validation rows, as `tools/shot_sweep_predict.py` computes it. The predict step reads validation rows and C's validation predictions only; it reads no test row. A cell's predicted label is "F beats C" if D/C*_val ≥ τ and "not distinguished" if D/C*_val ≤ 0.05; otherwise there is no prediction. The thresholds are τ = 0.189 at N1 and τ = 0.100 at N2. They are the medians, over the six earlier rows, of the shot-sweep rule's "adds" thresholds max(0.10, 2h). A predicted "not distinguished" matches an observed "C beats F"; a predicted "F beats C" does not. A cell is label-changing when its predicted label differs from the observed label of the same row and rung at 2,048 shots ("C beats F" read as "not distinguished"). Over the six levels other than 2,048, P6 passes if at least 80 percent of predicted cells and at least 80 percent of label-changing cells carry the predicted label. It is not testable if fewer than six cells are label-changing or they come from fewer than two rows. On the earlier seeds, this scoring gives 59 of 63 predicted cells and 23 of 26 label-changing cells. The kept-label baseline predicts the 2,048-shot label at every level; it is reported on the same cells. On the earlier seeds it matched 39 of 63 predicted cells and 3 of 26 label-changing cells.

**P7, the shot rule tracks the size of the gap.** Over the cells of P6 at the six levels other than 2,048 with D/C*_val ≥ 0.10, the median ratio of observed D/C to D/C*_val lies between 0.6 and 1.1. In at most 10 percent of these cells does the lower limit of the observed D/C interval exceed D/C*_val. P7 needs at least ten such cells from at least two rows; otherwise it is not testable. On the earlier seeds the median was 0.841 over 48 cells, with no exceedance.

**P8, the R0 stacking increment (original candidates).** At R0 and the exact level, the interval of the relative stacking increment of r over the recalibrated C lies above zero on every row. On the earlier seeds the six intervals ran from [0.133, 0.216] to [0.363, 0.453].

The predictions file also gives D/C*_val at R0 and R5 at every level, and the paper reports it beside the observed D/C there. Neither rung enters P6 or P7.

## Reading

`tools/fresh_confirmation.py score` writes one record per prediction with its outcome, the fresh value, and the earlier seeds' value. The paper reports every prediction in the main text, in one table beside the statement it tests, whatever the outcomes.
1. A passing prediction allows the paper to say that the statement held on three dataset seeds generated after this rule was frozen.
2. A failing prediction requires the paper to state the statement for the earlier seeds and the fresh result beside it, wherever the statement appears; no fresh cell is left out.
3. A prediction that is not testable is reported with the count that fell short.

Each prediction supports only its own statement. P1 asks that no R0 cell reads "F beats C", so low power alone can pass it; the paper reads it together with P2. P2 bounds the R0 effect for the strong candidates at 2,048 shots only. A claim that measurements add nothing at R0 at other levels or for the original candidates therefore rests on the labels alone and says so. P5 is mainly a check that the positive controls still read "F beats C". P7 bounds the median ratio, so single cells can still miss by more.

The fresh seeds do not replace the earlier records, and no decision pools fresh and earlier seeds. A pooled value may be reported as descriptive. No outcome changes a prediction, a threshold, a rung, a level, or a candidate set. Any analysis chosen after the fresh results are seen is labeled post hoc.

## Reported Beside the Predictions (Descriptive)

- Every fresh cell's labels, D, D/C, and intervals, for both fit sets, with the earlier seeds' values.
- The strong-candidate derived baseline against the original-candidate fits at 2,048 shots, which are expected to be bit-identical on the same platform; any difference is reported.
- C and A identity across levels within each fit set, as in the shot-sweep rule.
- F − R at every cell by `tools/descriptor_ladder_posthoc.py --part A`, the seed-ensemble estimand D_ens by `tools/learner_adequacy.py`, and selection counts per arm and row.
- The stacking increments at every rung and level that the stacking script covers.

## Deviations and Release

Any deviation from this rule is reported with its reason. On DeltaAI, `blas_fpe_probe()` runs before the first fit; matrix-multiplication warnings count as spurious in the sensitivity analysis only if that probe reproduces them on finite inputs. All fits are used. The release adds the fresh datasets, every level's caches, every fit with its per-candidate predictions, the predictions file, the run log, and the generation commands. In the paper, this panel appears as a confirmation whose rule was frozen before its circuits were generated, motivated by review.
