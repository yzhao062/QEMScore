# Frozen Rule: Round-9 Follow-Ups (Exact References and Descriptors for ML-QEM, a Bayes Oracle for the Descriptor Ladder, Six-Seed Pooling, and Field Baselines)

Written 2026-10-06 (US Pacific time), before any computation this rule governs. The eighth internal review panel prompted the five parts below. Every computation in them starts only after this rule and the code it names are pushed to the public repository.

## Known When This Rule Was Written

- **ML-QEM labels are sampled.** At commit `b1eccf8`, `docs/tutorials/h13_ising_data_gen.ipynb` computes each circuit's "ideal" values with a noiseless `QasmSimulator` at 10,000 shots and `cal_z_exp(counts)`, not from a statevector. The paper's Experiment 4 (rule `2026-10-04-mlqem-own-data.md`) scored every arm against these sampled references.
- **A referee's check on the test split.** During the panel review, a reviewer computed analytic values for the 300 zero-step circuits of the no-readout test split. It reported a reference error of 0.007585 against analytic values. Its random-forest control and full method scored 0.007613 and 0.008227 against the archived references, and 0.000883 and 0.002835 against analytic values. No other test-split quantity against exact labels was known.
- **The authors' check on training files only.** A reference implementation was run on five training files: no-readout steps 0, 1, and 9, readout step 5, and coherent step 14. Archived minus exact had a mean absolute value of 0.0072 to 0.0078. z-scores against the 10,000-shot binomial standard deviation had SD 0.95 to 1.00. J was recovered in [0, 1] and was identical within each circuit, and the measurement basis was recoverable. The code then ran on all 13,500 training circuits of the three settings. The SD of z was 0.987 to 1.003 and |mean z| at most 0.0013. The largest |z| was 4.64, the only entry above 4. One training circuit (no readout error, step 8, entry 132) has no CX sandwich: the transpiler removed its near-zero ZZ rotations and cancelled the CX pairs.
- **Oracle development on validation circuits.** The oracle code was developed on the validation circuits of dataset seed 101 at 2,048 shots, used as a pseudo-test set. Its surrogates were fitted on training circuits only. The same validation circuits selected the ridge alpha and supplied the development risks, so those values served selection as well as evaluation. Surrogate validation errors were 2.4e-6 to 3.9e-4. The shot-noise z-scores had SD 1.000. G*/C* was 0.017, 0.148, 0.474, 0.722, 0.818, and 0.812 for transverse-field Ising at N1 to N4, R3-TFI, and R5. For Heisenberg at N1 to N4, R3-Heis, and R5 it was 0.020, 0.094, 0.262, 0.371, 0.079, and 0.385. The G* interval of transverse-field Ising N1 included zero. All these development values conditioned on each item's actual noise strength, L1 or L3; no development value exists for the hidden-strength oracle below. Part C's expectations were written with these values known. No oracle quantity was computed on a test circuit.
- **Spin-chain results.** Every analysis file under `artifacts/descriptor-information/` is public. Each per-seed label, D, and interval that Parts C and D read was therefore known. The Bayes risks, pooled estimates, margins, and their readings were not computed.
- **Field results.** `reanalysis/outputs/qlear/field-report.json` and the QRAFT reports are public. The 41 per-fit prediction files behind the Q-LEAR report were retrieved from the machine that fitted them. Rescoring them reproduces the packaged report to 1.2e-16. No raw-arm score, opposite-order fit, or Shapley share was computed.

## Part A: ML-QEM Against Exact References

**Exact labels.** `python -m reanalysis.mlqem.exact --frozen-rule` (this file) covers every circuit of every split of the three settings of the 2026-10-04 rule. It computes the four labels from the statevector of the transpiled circuit without its final measurements. Each label is P(1) − P(0) of the qubit measured into clbit n − 1 − i, the order and sign of `cal_z_exp`. Every data file must match `reanalysis/mlqem/upstream_data_sha256.json`. It runs in the ML-QEM environment of the 2026-10-04 rule, on these splits: no readout error, `train`, `val`, `val_extra`; readout error, `train`, `val`, `val_Zonly`; coherent over-rotation, `train`, `val`. Outputs go to `artifacts/mlqem-exact/`.

**Gate.** For each setting and split, z = (archived − exact)/sqrt((1 − exact²)/10,000) over entries with 1 − exact² ≥ 1e-6. Entries below that bound must have archived equal to exact up to 1e-12. The gate passes if, for every setting and split, |mean z| ≤ 0.1, the SD of z lies in [0.9, 1.1], and no |z| exceeds 6. `python -m reanalysis.mlqem.exact gate` applies these thresholds to all eight setting and split manifests and writes `artifacts/mlqem-exact/gate.json`. The rescoring and the Part B fits refuse to start unless that file passes and its recorded array hashes match the arrays they load. If the gate fails, Parts A and B are reported as failed with the gate values, and no rescoring is read.

**Rescoring.** `reanalysis/mlqem/analyze.py --targets exact --frozen-rule` (this file) `--fit-frozen-rule docs/frozen-rules/2026-10-04-mlqem-own-data.md` scores the round-8 fits (release `confirmation-v1`, asset `mlqem-own-data-v1`) against the exact labels. Before scoring, it asserts that each fit's stored targets equal the archived labels, cast to the fit's precision, and that the circuit identifiers and steps agree in order. Fit binding uses the 2026-10-04 rule's hash; the output records both rules' hashes. The output is `artifacts/mlqem-own-data/analysis-exact-targets.json`. Estimands, bootstrap, seeds, and labels are those of the 2026-10-04 rule. It adds C − R and F − R with intervals, and the reference error: the mean over test circuits of the mean absolute archived − exact. The reproduction check stays on the archived references, where the published error was measured; the round-8 analysis keeps it.

**Expectations stated in advance (not decision rules).**
1. The gate passes.
2. In the primary cell (no readout error, random forest), D/C against exact labels is at least D/C against archived references (points).
3. Every cell keeps its round-8 label, "F beats C".
4. F − R lies below zero in every cell.

**Reading.** The paper's estimand is defined against exact labels, so the paper reports the exact-label values as Experiment 4's result. It reports the archived-reference values beside them as the reproduction of the published pipeline. A label that differs between the two is reported with both values.

## Part B: ML-QEM With Exact Descriptors

**Fits.** As in the 2026-10-04 rule (settings, models, arms F, C, P, R, Rcal, learner seeds 1 to 20, one OLS fit for F and C), with one change. `--descriptors exact` appends five columns to the descriptor block of F, C, and P. They are the coupling J, a one-hot code of the measurement basis (three columns), and the step count. J is 0 at step 0, where no coupling enters the circuit. `reanalysis/mlqem/exact.py` extracts J and the basis from each transpiled circuit. J is minus the rz angle between two identical CX gates (the angle is −2·J·dt with dt = 0.5). The basis is the Pauli U†ZU of the single-qubit gates between the last two barriers. A circuit above step 0 with no sandwich gets J = 0 and is listed in the output. The transpiler removed its near-zero ZZ rotations, so the executed circuit is the J = 0 circuit. Extraction stops on any other circuit whose sandwich count is not three per step, whose angles disagree by more than 1e-9, or whose four measured qubits disagree on the basis. Training and validation targets stay the archived labels, as published. The runner asserts that each exact array's archived labels equal the encoded labels row by row. The command is `python -m reanalysis.mlqem.run --descriptors exact --exact-dir artifacts/mlqem-exact --frozen-rule` (this file), with the settings, models, arms, and seeds above.

**Secondary fits.** The primary cell (no readout error, random forest) gets two more sets of F and C fits at learner seeds 1 to 20. Both use `--train-targets exact`, so their training and validation targets are the exact labels. One set uses the published encoding and writes to `artifacts/mlqem-own-data/round9/exact-training-encoding/fits`. The other uses the exact descriptors and writes to `.../exact-training-exact/fits`. `analyze.py --analysis-arms F C --settings no_readout --models rf` scores each against exact and archived targets with the same bootstrap. It reports C, F, D, D/C, their intervals, the label, and the reference error. Every fit must record `train_targets` as exact. These fits separate sampled-label noise from descriptor content.

**Scoring.** Against exact labels (primary) and archived references (secondary), with the estimands and bootstrap of Part A.

**Expectation stated in advance (not a decision rule).** In the primary cell, D/C against exact labels falls below Part A's value for the published encoding (points). No label is predicted.

**Reading.** J, the basis, and the step count determine each exact label, because the initial state is the same fixed circuit for all items. The Bayes information gain from the noisy values is therefore zero, as in the paper's Experiment 1. Any positive D reflects differences between the learned arms' finite-sample errors, including sampled-label noise; the secondary fits show how much of it remains with exact training labels. Part A's D measures the published encoding's fitted-pipeline gain at the declared learner and training size. The paper reports the descriptor settings side by side and treats no D as a bound on the Bayes information gain.

## Part C: A Bayes Oracle for the Spin-Chain Descriptor Ladder

**Data.** The spin-chain datasets of dataset seeds 101, 211, 307 (release `descriptor-information-v1`, shot-sweep asset) and 401, 503, 607 (release `confirmation-v1`) at every shot level.

**Surrogates.** For each dataset seed and family, `tools/bayes_oracle.py` fits degree-5 ridge polynomials on the couplings scaled to [−1, 1]. It fits one for the label y per observable, and one for the exact noisy expectation e per observable and noise strength, with e from the exact-level items. Alpha comes from {1e-12, ..., 1e-2} by validation-circuit error after fitting on training circuits; the chosen surrogate is refitted on training and validation circuits. Its test error is reported.

**Posteriors and predictions.** Couplings are uniform on [0.2, 1.2]. At N1 to N4 the posterior of each coupling is Normal(x′, s²) truncated to that interval, where x′ is the descriptor the fits read. At R3-TFI h is uniform and j exact; at R3-Heis jz is uniform and jx, jy exact; at R5 every coupling is uniform. At R0 the label is a function of the descriptors, so C* = F* = 0 by construction, and the surrogate's error is reported in its place. Each test circuit gets 20,000 posterior draws (R5: one shared set of 200,000 prior draws), seeded by `SeedSequence([20261006, dataset_seed, circuit_index, rung_index])`. The C* prediction is the posterior median of ŷ(J). The F* prediction at n shots is the posterior median weighted by the likelihood of the item's own r. Two variants share the same coupling draws. The observed-strength oracle uses Normal(r; ê(J), (1 − ê(J)²)/n) for the item's own noise strength. The hidden-strength oracle gives L1 and L3 equal prior weight and averages their two Normal likelihoods, each with its own ê and variance. C* is the same in both, because the label does not depend on the noise strength. An item whose effective sample size falls below 50 is redrawn with ten times as many draws, separately for each variant. Each mapping entry records whether its fits read the noise-strength indicator. `tools/bayes_oracle_strength_evidence.json` binds that flag to one C and one F record of the released fits, by file name and SHA-256. A derived set binds to its own derived fits. `tools/build_oracle_strength_evidence.py` writes that file. A missing record, a disagreeing flag, or evidence built from another mapping file stops the oracle. Its Equation 2 and confusion rows use the matching variant.

**Risks.** C*, F*, G* = C* − F*, and G*/C*, as the analysis's macro mean absolute error over a row's four cells. Intervals come from a circuit bootstrap (10,000 draws, seed 20261002, whole circuits). Levels: 2,048 shots at R0, N1 to N4, R3-TFI, R3-Heis, and R5 on all six seeds; N1 and N2 also at 256, 1,024, 8,192, 32,768, and 131,072 shots.

**Running.** `python tools/bayes_oracle.py --eval-split test --levels 256 1024 2048 8192 32768 131072 --out artifacts/descriptor-information/round9/bayes-oracle.json --summary-out artifacts/descriptor-information/round9/bayes-oracle.md --frozen-rule` (this file). The fit-set mapping is `tools/bayes_oracle_mapping.json`. The script takes its coupling names and noise scales from `tools/descriptor_ladder.py` and stops if they differ.

**Equation 2.** The mapping committed with the script lists the fit sets: original, strength-indicator, strong, and early-stopped candidates, the shot sweep, and the new seeds. For each fit set and level, the script reads C, F, D, and the label. It reports excess_C = C − C* and excess_F = F − F*, and checks D = G* + excess_C − excess_F. The mapping lists every expected (fit set, level, seed, family, rung) tuple through its dataset seeds and expected rungs per family. The script validates this inventory first: each tuple must appear exactly once, and a missing or duplicate row or rung stops it. The included tuples are those the oracle schedules. R0 at the exact level is included with C* = F* = 0 by construction; it needs no likelihood. Three kinds of mapped tuple are excluded before any outcome. Other rungs at the exact level fall outside the finite-shot likelihood implementation, R4 has no analytic posterior, and rungs other than N1 and N2 are scheduled only at 2,048 shots. The script lists every excluded tuple with its reason and stops if an included tuple, file, row, or field is missing.

**Confusion table.** Truth per row, rung, and level: "no information" at R0; "information" where the G* interval lies above zero; "undetermined" otherwise. For each fit set it tabulates truth against label. It counts false positives ("F beats C" with no information), misses ("not distinguished" with information), and wrong signs ("C beats F" with information). Outside R0, the oracle uses fitted surrogates and finite Monte Carlo draws. Its circuit bootstrap conditions on those approximations. The 1e-3 flag does not certify accuracy relative to G*. Information classifications outside R0 are therefore approximate, and the paper calls them surrogate-assisted oracle estimates.

**Expectations stated in advance (not decision rules).**
1. Every surrogate's test error is below 1e-3. A surrogate at or above it is flagged, and every oracle, Equation 2, and confusion entry it enters carries the flag.
2. At every finite level, the z-scores of r − e against (1 − e²)/n have SD in [0.9, 1.1].
3. At 2,048 shots the G* interval lies above zero at N2, N3, N4, R3-TFI, R3-Heis, and R5 on every row.
4. G*/C* rises from N1 to N4 on every row (points).
5. The hidden-strength G* is at most the observed-strength G* on every row and level (points). Expectations 3 and 4 concern the observed-strength oracle.

**Reading.** G* is the gain the measurement can give at that rung and level, given the descriptors. The paper reports the Equation 2 terms and the confusion table. A label of "F beats C" at R0 is a false positive relative to information. "Not distinguished" where G* lies above zero is a miss. A miss is a statement about the power of the fitted pipelines.

## Part D: Six-Seed Pooling and an Absolute-Scale Margin

**Inputs.** The per-seed D and its two-stage bootstrap SD (`draw_sd`) for dataset seeds 101, 211, 307, 401, 503, and 607 in three fit sets. Every fit reads the noise-strength indicator. The sets are the original candidates at 2,048 shots, the strong candidates at 2,048 shots, and the strong candidates at the exact level. For seeds 101, 211, and 307, the original candidates come from the strength-indicator rerun, `artifacts/descriptor-information/strength-indicator/analysis-a.json`. Each set pools every rung present for all six seeds. The tool stops if any input comes from fits without the indicator, so no pool mixes descriptor policies.

**Pooling.** `tools/pooled_absolute.py` computes the DerSimonian–Laird between-seed variance, the random-effects estimate, and a Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman 95 percent interval on 5 degrees of freedom, with I² and the fixed-effect estimate. The pooled label uses the three-way rule of the earlier rules on that interval.

**Running.** `python tools/pooled_absolute.py --out artifacts/descriptor-information/round9/pooled-absolute.json --out-md artifacts/descriptor-information/round9/pooled-absolute.md --frozen-rule` (this file), which reads six analysis files, two per fit set, and the 2,048-shot datasets of the six seeds.

**Margin.** Rcal is the calibrated raw estimate, as the measurement-floor analysis defines it. For each family, delta = 0.1 × the mean over the six seeds of Rcal's macro test error at 2,048 shots, applied at every level. A pooled D reads as a "practically relevant gain" (lower limit above delta), a "practically relevant loss" (upper limit below −delta), "practically negligible" (interval inside (−delta, delta)), or "undetermined". The factor 0.1 follows the threshold the shot-sweep rule used for D/C; it is fixed here, before any pooled value or margin is computed.

**Expectations stated in advance (not decision rules).**
1. Original candidates with the strength indicator, 2,048 shots, R0: the pooled interval contains zero for both families.
2. Strong candidates, exact level, R0: the pooled D is practically negligible for both families.
3. R5 reads a practically relevant gain in every fit set that has it.

**Reading.** The pooled labels and margins are reported beside the frozen rung statements; they do not replace them.

## Part E: Field Baselines and the Opposite Ladder Order

**Archive.** The 41 per-fit prediction files of the Q-LEAR ladder go into `reanalysis/outputs/qlear/fits/` with their SHA-256 values. A check rescoring them reproduces `field-report.json`.

**Raw arms.** Q-LEAR: arm R predicts each state's observed probability from the same evaluation file. It is scored with the frozen Hellinger, normalization, macro, and bootstrap of the learned arms, with F − R and C − R. QRAFT: arm R predicts each state row's observed forward-run probability (`StateUpProb50`), scored with the panel's metric on the panel's ten splits. On Panel M, each arm's error and each learned arm minus R are means over the ten seeds. Their intervals come from a joint row bootstrap with 10,000 draws and seed 20261002. Each draw resamples test rows once, stratified by machine, applies the draw to every seed's split, and averages the per-seed values. Arm errors and contrasts share the draws. Panel R scores one released test table with its existing machine-stratified row bootstrap.

Before scoring, the raw arm, the archived arms, and O2 must have identical target vectors and supports per panel and file.

**Opposite order.** Arm O2 reads descriptors, state weight, and the three inverted-error columns (DPE). It uses the recipe of the other network arms at seeds 0 to 9, in the environment and on the machine of the original fits (Python 3.10, `environments/requirements-qlear.txt`). The ladder C → O2 → F gives the opposite-order increments. The order-averaged (two-group Shapley) shares of base execution and DPE in C − F get intervals from the scorer's bootstrap draws.

**Running.** `reanalysis/scripts/qlear/fit_field_half.py <release> reanalysis/outputs/qlear/fits --arm O2 --seed k --frozen-rule` (this file) runs once for each k from 0 to 9. O2 writes its own manifest; the archived files and their checksums stay unchanged, and the legacy scorer ignores O2 files. `score_field_half.py reanalysis/outputs/qlear/fits reanalysis/outputs/qlear/round9 --release <release> --raw --opposite --frozen-rule` (this file) scores them. `score_qraft_panel.py <release> reanalysis/outputs/qraft/round9 --raw --frozen-rule` (this file) scores QRAFT. The published `field-report.json` and QRAFT reports stay unchanged.

**Expectations stated in advance (not decision rules).**
1. Q-LEAR's F has a lower hardware macro error than R.
2. On QRAFT, the learned arm with the lowest error has a lower error than R.

**Reading.** These are consistency checks on released data, read as in the paper's Section 9.

## Deviations and Release

Any deviation is reported with its reason. No outcome changes a setting, model, arm, rung, level, margin, or split. Any analysis chosen after these results are seen is labeled post hoc. The release adds the exact-label arrays and manifests, the Part B fits, the oracle and pooling outputs, the O2 fits, and the run logs. The ML-QEM inputs are released only in derived form (encoded matrices, labels, and predictions) under the upstream Apache-2.0 license, with the commit and file SHA-256 values identifying the original files.
